Phase 1 · Menu setup

Build your catalog foundation

Menus are the source of truth for everything buyers see — POS, kiosks, online ordering, and delivery apps all pull from the same catalog. Build it right here first.

Choose your build method
Upload, import, AI starter, or build manually
Done

Square gives you four ways to build your menu. The right choice depends on whether you're starting from scratch, migrating from another system, or already have a spreadsheet of items ready.

A
CSV Upload — fastest for migrations

Export your current menu as a spreadsheet, map columns to Square's template (Name, Price, Category, Description), then upload under Items → Import. Square validates each row and flags errors before saving.

B
AI Starter Menu — best for new sellers

Go to Items → Menus → Create menu → AI starter. Type your restaurant concept and cuisine style. Square generates a complete starting menu with items, descriptions, and suggested prices. Edit before publishing.

C
Manual build — most control

Go to Items → Create item. Add name, price, category, description, and image for each item one at a time. Best for sellers with fewer than 30 items or highly customized catalogs.

D
Import from another platform

If the seller is on Toast, Clover, or Lightspeed, use Items → Import → Connect source. Square maps common fields automatically. Always review category structure after import — it rarely transfers perfectly.

Before you importClean up your source data first. Remove duplicate items, standardize price formatting (no $ signs in CSV cells), and ensure every item has a category. Garbage in, garbage out.
Pro tipEven if you use AI starter or CSV import, plan to spend 30 minutes reviewing item descriptions. Generic descriptions reduce add-on attach rate. Specific ones ("house-made, slow-braised for 6 hours") drive higher order values.
Step checklist
  • Review existing item list or POS export before starting
  • Select and complete your build method
  • Review item names, descriptions, and prices after build

Create menu groups and modifiers
Categories, sub-groups, modifier sets, and item pairing
Done

Menu groups control how items are organized on every buyer-facing surface. Items without a group will not appear on online ordering, kiosks, or delivery apps — this is the most commonly skipped step.

1
Create your top-level groups

Go to Items → Menus → [your menu] → Add group. Typical QSR structure: Starters, Mains, Sides, Drinks, Desserts. Keep it to 6–8 groups max — more than that overwhelms buyers on mobile.

2
Assign every item to a group

Drag items into groups from the menu builder, or use the bulk-assign tool under Items → Manage items → Assign to menu. Filter by "unassigned" to catch anything missed.

3
Build modifier groups

Go to Items → Modifiers → Create modifier set. Common sets: Size (Small/Medium/Large), Temperature (Hot/Iced), Add-ons (Extra shot, Oat milk). Set min/max selections and mark required vs optional. Then attach the modifier set to relevant items.

4
Set availability windows

For items only available at certain times (Breakfast Menu, Happy Hour), use Item availability → Schedule on the item page. This prevents off-menu orders without manual toggling.

Critical: Items must be added to a menu group to appear on online ordering, kiosks, and delivery apps. A complete item catalog with no groups assigned = zero buyer-facing visibility.
Modifiers drive revenueWell-structured modifier sets (especially upsell add-ons) meaningfully increase average order value. Spend time here — it pays off.
Step checklist
  • Create all top-level menu groups (max 6–8)
  • Assign every item to at least one group
  • Build and attach modifier sets for applicable items
  • Confirm no items are left unassigned
Assign menus to sales channels
POS, online ordering, kiosk, and delivery apps
Done

One catalog can power multiple surfaces with different visibility rules. A "Lunch Menu" might appear on online ordering and POS but not on DoorDash. Channel assignment is where you control that.

1
Assign to POS mode

Go to Point of Sale → Settings → Menu and select the menu for each POS mode (e.g. Dine-in vs Takeout). Servers will see exactly this menu when they open that mode.

2
Assign to online ordering

In Online → Overview → Menu settings, connect the menu to your ordering profile. Only items in connected menus will appear on your ordering page. If nothing appears after setup, check this first.

3
Assign to kiosk (if applicable)

Under Square Kiosk → Settings → Menu. Kiosk menus typically exclude items that require staff customization — keep the kiosk menu leaner than the POS menu.

4
Verify buyer-facing preview

Open your ordering URL in an incognito window and browse every group. Confirm prices, item names, and images look right before moving on. This is your buyer's first impression.

Step checklist
  • Assign menu to each POS mode in use
  • Assign menu to online ordering channel
  • Preview ordering URL and verify all groups visible
QA your catalog before going live
Prices, images, tax, and an end-to-end order test
Done

Before any buyer-facing channel goes live, run this QA pass. It takes 20 minutes and prevents the most common activation issues.

1
Check prices and tax settings

In Items → Taxes, confirm the correct tax rate is applied to each item or category. Verify that modifiers carry correct pricing (especially "upcharge" modifiers).

2
Add images to high-traffic items

Items with photos convert at significantly higher rates on online ordering. Upload at least for your top 10–15 sellers. Use square images (1:1 ratio), minimum 600×600px. Avoid dark or cluttered backgrounds.

3
Place a test order end-to-end

Open POS, ring up 3–5 items including at least one with modifiers. Complete the transaction. Confirm receipt prints (or sends digitally) and the order appears in Dashboard under Transactions.

Note: Test transactions processed via Square's test card won't appear in live reports. Use a real card for your final verification order, then issue a refund immediately after.
Step checklist
  • Verify all prices and tax settings
  • Add photos to top 10–15 items
  • Place and complete a test POS order
  • Confirm transaction shows in Dashboard

Create your online ordering profile
Branded direct ordering — no marketplace fees
Done

The ordering profile is the branded page buyers land on when ordering directly. It pulls your menu catalog automatically — but a published menu must exist before setup begins.

Prerequisite: Phase 1 must be complete. You cannot set up online ordering without a published, channel-assigned menu.
1
Open Online Ordering setup

From Square Dashboard: Online → Get started or Online → Overview → Edit profile if already initialized.

2
Set your business details

Add your restaurant name, logo, cover photo, and cuisine type. These appear on the order page and in search results. Use high-quality images — this is your digital storefront.

3
Configure fulfillment types

Enable Pickup, Delivery, or both. For delivery, set your delivery radius, minimum order amount, and delivery fee. For pickup, set your prep time (most QSRs use 10–15 min).

4
Set ordering hours

Configure hours separately from your general business hours if needed. Online ordering can have narrower windows — useful for sellers with limited kitchen staff.

5
Publish and test

Click Publish. Copy your ordering URL, open in incognito, place a test order to end. Confirm the order appears in Dashboard → Orders and an email confirmation is sent.

Step checklist
  • Confirm published menu exists from Phase 1
  • Add business name, logo, cover image
  • Configure fulfillment (pickup and/or delivery settings)
  • Set ordering hours and prep time
  • Publish and place a test order end-to-end
Embed ordering on your website
Order Online vs Shop All — and how to choose
Done

Square gives you two ways to bring ordering into your website. F&B sellers should almost always use "Order Online" — "Shop All" is designed for retail product browsing, not restaurant ordering.

1
Decide: Order Online vs Shop All

Order Online: Routes buyers through your Square ordering profile. Best for restaurants, food trucks, cafes. Supports pickup/delivery, schedules, and prep times.
Shop All: Displays a product grid for browsing. Best for retail sellers with large catalogs. Not recommended for F&B.

2
Generate your embed code

Go to Online → Order page → Share → Embed. Copy the snippet. It's a simple <a> tag that opens your ordering profile — no iframe or complex integration needed.

3
Place on your website

Add the button to your homepage header or nav, and again on your Contact/Find Us page. Make it a primary CTA — don't bury it. If you use WordPress, Squarespace, or Wix, paste in the Custom HTML block.

4
Test the full web-to-order flow

On mobile and desktop: land on your website → click Order Online → browse menu → add items → checkout. Confirm the order completes and appears in Dashboard.

Mobile-first: Over 70% of online food orders are placed on mobile. Test your ordering flow on a phone before declaring it live — button size, image loading, and checkout UX on mobile are the top friction points.
Step checklist
  • Choose the correct page type (Order Online for F&B)
  • Generate embed code and add to website
  • Test full flow on mobile and desktop
Connect DoorDash (if in scope)
Delivery marketplace sync and ongoing management
Done

Only complete this step if DoorDash activation is part of the seller's launch plan. Once connected, Square keeps menus in sync automatically — item updates in Square flow to DoorDash within minutes.

1
Confirm prerequisites

A Square menu must be built and published. The seller must have an active DoorDash merchant account. If they don't have one, they'll need to sign up at merchant.doordash.com before connecting.

2
Connect in Square Dashboard

Go to App Marketplace → DoorDash → Connect. Log in with DoorDash merchant credentials. Select which menu to sync. The initial sync takes 10–30 minutes.

3
Review synced menu on DoorDash

Log into DoorDash merchant portal and confirm menu structure, images, and pricing transferred correctly. Pay special attention to modifiers — complex nested modifier groups sometimes need manual cleanup.

4
Test a DoorDash order

Place a test order from the DoorDash consumer app to confirm it flows through to Square POS as an incoming order. Check that order tickets print and the order appears in Dashboard.

Menu sync is one-way: Changes made directly in the DoorDash portal will be overwritten the next time Square syncs. All menu edits must be made in Square to persist.
Step checklist
  • Confirm DoorDash merchant account exists
  • Connect via Square App Marketplace
  • Review synced menu in DoorDash portal
  • Place and verify a DoorDash test order

Account setup and permissions
Owner access, team roles, location structure
Done

Getting account access and team permissions right from day one prevents most support escalations. Spend time here — fixing permissions after go-live is painful for everyone.

1
Verify owner access to the Square account

The business owner should be the account owner — not a rep, not a manager. Go to Account → Account & Settings → Personal information. Confirm name, email, and phone match the business owner.

2
Set up your location(s)

Each physical location needs its own Square location profile. Go to Account → Business information → Locations → Add location. Set address, timezone, and business hours for each. Multi-location sellers: menus, devices, and reporting are all location-specific.

3
Create team permission sets

Go to Team → Permissions → Create permission set. Build roles before inviting staff. Typical restaurant setup: Owner (full access), Manager (reports + voids + refunds), Server (ring sales + view menu only). Never give manager-level access to all staff by default.

4
Invite team members

Go to Team → Team members → Invite. Assign each person a permission set, location access, and wage (required for timecards). Staff get an email invite to create their own login pin.

5
Enable two-factor authentication

Require 2FA for the owner account under Account → Security → Two-step verification. This protects payouts and account settings from unauthorized changes.

Security risk: Sharing the owner login pin with staff is the single most common cause of unauthorized refunds and account changes. Always create individual logins for each team member.
Step checklist
  • Confirm owner is the Square account owner
  • Create and configure all location profiles
  • Create permission sets (Owner, Manager, Server)
  • Invite all team members with correct roles
  • Enable two-factor authentication on owner account
POS app setup and configuration
Download, sign in, POS mode, device codes, passcodes
Done

The Square POS app needs to be installed, signed in, and configured with the correct mode before any hardware is connected. This is the software-side of device setup.

1
Download the Square app

On each device (iPad or Android tablet): download Square Point of Sale from the App Store or Google Play. Do not download Square for Restaurants or Square for Retail unless that's the intended product — wrong app is a common source of confusion.

2
Sign in with a device code

From Dashboard: Devices → Device codes → Create code. Name the device (e.g. "Front Counter 1"), assign a location, and generate the code. On the device, open Square → Sign in with device code → enter the 6-character code. This links the device to the correct location without sharing the owner password.

3
Select POS mode

After signing in, Square prompts you to choose a mode: Quick Service (counter orders, no table management), Full Service (table layout, coursing, server assignment), or Retail. For QSR, select Quick Service. For sit-down, select Full Service.

4
Set passcodes for each staff member

Each team member needs their own 4-digit passcode to clock in and take payments. Go to Team → [Employee] → Edit → Set passcode. Confirm each person can sign in on the device before training day.

5
Configure device-level settings

In the POS app: Settings → Hardware — connect receipt printer and cash drawer. Settings → Checkout — set tip options, signature thresholds, and receipt delivery default. Settings → Notifications — enable online order alerts.

Payment processing setup
Bank account, payout schedule, and processing verification
Done

Payments won't reach the seller's bank until this is configured. This is often completed during sign-up, but always verify before go-live — unverified accounts can have processing holds placed.

1
Complete identity verification

Square requires identity verification before processing live payments. Go to Account → Account & Settings → Business → Legal details. Provide owner SSN or EIN, date of birth, and business address. Verification is instant in most cases.

2
Connect a bank account

Go to Account → Balance → Bank accounts → Add bank account. Enter routing and account number, or use Plaid for instant verification. Payouts go here — double-check the account number carefully.

3
Set payout schedule

Default is next-business-day payouts. Sellers can switch to daily (Instant Transfer, fee applies) or weekly under Account → Balance → Transfer settings. Help the seller understand the difference before they go live.

4
Process a real verification transaction

Run a $1.00 real transaction and refund it. Confirm it settles in the connected bank account within 1–2 business days. If it doesn't, flag for support before go-live — not after the first real shift.

Don't skip this: If the seller goes live without verifying their bank account, payouts will be held in Square Balance. This is the most common "where's my money?" support call after activation.
Step checklist
  • Complete identity verification (SSN or EIN)
  • Connect and verify bank account
  • Set preferred payout schedule
  • Process and confirm a $1 real verification transaction
Integrations and add-on apps
Loyalty, marketing, accounting, and kitchen display
Done

Square's App Marketplace connects to 300+ third-party tools. Activate only what the seller needs before go-live — adding too many apps at once creates confusion during training. These four are the most commonly needed.

A
Square Kitchen Display System (KDS)

If the kitchen has a screen instead of a printer: App Marketplace → Square KDS → Enable. Download the Square KDS app on the kitchen device and sign in with a device code. Set ticket routing rules (which items go to which screen).

B
Square Loyalty

Go to Loyalty → Get started. Configure how customers earn points (per visit, per dollar spent, or per item). Set reward thresholds. Loyalty prompts automatically appear at checkout on POS and online ordering — no extra hardware needed.

C
Square Marketing

Connect under Marketing → Get started. Import the customer list from previous platform if available. Set up a welcome email campaign to go out when loyalty is first activated. This takes 20 minutes but drives measurable return visits in the first 90 days.

D
Accounting integration (QuickBooks or Xero)

Under App Marketplace → [QuickBooks/Xero] → Connect. Authorize the connection and map Square transaction categories to the seller's chart of accounts. Test by processing a transaction and confirming it syncs within 24 hours.

Prioritize KDS and Loyalty: If the seller only has time for two integrations before go-live, make it these. KDS reduces order errors. Loyalty drives repeat business from day one.
Step checklist
  • Set up Square KDS (if kitchen display is in scope)
  • Enable and configure Square Loyalty
  • Connect accounting integration (if applicable)
  • Verify all integrations work with a test transaction

Pre-kickoff preparation
Align on scope, hardware, and logistics before the call
Done

A good kickoff call starts with homework done on both sides. Run through this checklist 48 hours before — surprises on the call day set the wrong tone and waste everyone's time.

1
Confirm hardware has shipped and arrival date

Check the hardware order in Dashboard or the carrier tracking email. If hardware hasn't shipped 5 days before kickoff, escalate immediately — don't wait until the day before.

2
Confirm Phases 1–3 are complete

Before the kickoff call: menus published, online ordering live, software fully configured. Training on a half-built system erodes seller confidence. Completion of earlier phases is a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have.

3
Send the seller a pre-call prep email

Ask them to: have their iPad or tablet charged, have all staff present for training, have their Wi-Fi password handy, and confirm their go-live date. This email reduces no-shows and day-of surprises by half.

4
Prepare your demo environment

If using the QSR Product Walkthrough demo account: log in the night before and verify the demo menu is populated. Nothing kills momentum like a demo account that's broken or empty on the day.

Step checklist
  • Confirm hardware shipped with expected delivery date
  • Verify Phases 1–3 are fully complete
  • Send seller pre-call prep email
  • Verify demo account and QSR walkthrough materials
POS and Dashboard training
Live walkthrough with the seller and their staff
Done

Run this as a hands-on session — staff should be on the actual device, not watching a screen share. Give them tasks to complete themselves, not just a demonstration to observe.

1
Opening the POS (10 min)

Each staff member signs in with their passcode. Walk through: how to select a POS mode, how the menu grid looks, how to search for an item, and how to navigate back to the home screen. Have each person do this themselves.

2
Taking an order (15 min)

Walk through a complete order: add items, apply modifiers, add a discount, remove an item. Then: select payment type (card / cash / split). Process a card payment. Print or send a receipt. Have two or three staff do this themselves before moving on.

3
Handling common situations (15 min)

Cover: how to void an item after ordering, how to process a refund, how to open the cash drawer manually, and how to apply a comp or house account. These are the four situations that will cause panic on day one if untrained.

4
Dashboard overview for manager/owner (15 min)

Focus on: End of Day report (how to run it and what to look at), Transactions view (how to find and review a specific sale), Team clock-ins, and how to update an item price. Don't go deep on every feature — they'll learn the rest by using it.

Training structure that works: Show once → have them do it → have a second person do it. Passive observation doesn't stick. Active repetition does. Budget 90 minutes minimum for a full staff of 5+.
Step checklist
  • All staff sign in with their own passcode on device
  • Each staff member completes a full order hands-on
  • Cover voids, refunds, cash drawer, and comps
  • Walk owner/manager through End of Day and reports

Online ordering operations training
Managing orders, updating hours, and handling 86s
Done

Online orders require a different operational muscle than in-person orders. This training is separate from POS training and should be run with the manager who will own online operations.

1
How online orders arrive in POS

When an online order is placed, the POS device shows an incoming order notification. Walk through: accepting the order, reviewing the items, printing the kitchen ticket. Show what happens if the order is not accepted within the timeout window (it auto-cancels and the customer is refunded).

2
Updating ordering hours

From Dashboard: Online → Overview → Edit hours. Show the manager how to temporarily pause ordering (for busy periods or equipment issues) by toggling "Pause ordering" — this is faster than changing hours and resuming when ready.

3
86ing items in real time

From POS: Items → [item] → Mark as sold out. This immediately removes the item from online ordering and kiosk. Sold-out items auto-restore at midnight unless manually re-enabled. Walk through this with the manager — it's used constantly during service.

4
Handling customer order issues

For refunds on online orders: go to Dashboard → Orders → [order] → Issue refund. The refund is automatic and returns to the original payment method within 5–7 days. Show the manager where to find the customer's contact info on the order if they need to reach out directly.

Step checklist
  • Demonstrate incoming online order flow in POS
  • Show how to update ordering hours and pause ordering
  • Walk through marking items sold out (86ing)
  • Demonstrate online order refund process
Final QA and go-live declaration
End-to-end test across every active channel
Done

Don't declare the seller live until every item on this list passes. A failed go-live on day one sets a damaging precedent and often leads to churn within 90 days.

1
POS order test

Ring up a 3-item order with at least one modifier, process with a real card, confirm receipt prints or sends digitally, and confirm the transaction shows in Dashboard → Transactions. Issue refund.

2
Online order test

Place an order from the seller's public ordering URL. Confirm it appears as an incoming order in POS. Accept it. Confirm the kitchen ticket prints (if KDS: verify it shows on screen). Confirm customer email confirmation was sent.

3
Hardware check

Receipt printer: test print. Cash drawer: open with correct tender type. Card reader: process a test swipe/tap. KDS screen (if applicable): confirm all ticket items appear and can be marked complete.

4
End-of-day close practice

Run through the close process with the manager: Dashboard → Reports → End of Day. Review the report together. Confirm they know how to reconcile cash drawer and where to find net sales. Document their close time so you can check in tomorrow.

After go-live: Schedule a check-in call or message for the end of the seller's first live day. Quick wins in the first 24 hours (high transaction count, no issues) make the difference between a seller who champions Square and one who becomes a churn risk.
Step checklist
  • Complete POS order test (card, receipt, Dashboard)
  • Complete online order test (submit → POS → kitchen)
  • Confirm all hardware working (printer, drawer, reader, KDS)
  • Run end-of-day close with manager
  • Schedule day-1 check-in call with seller
